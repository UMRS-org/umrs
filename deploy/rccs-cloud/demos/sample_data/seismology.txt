Seismic wave propagation in heterogeneous media requires accurate numerical methods.
The finite-difference method is widely used for solving the elastic wave equation on structured grids.
P-waves (primary waves) are compressional waves that travel faster than S-waves through Earth's interior.
S-waves (secondary waves) are shear waves that cannot propagate through fluids.
Full waveform inversion (FWI) is an iterative technique that minimizes the misfit between observed and synthetic seismograms.
GPU acceleration has dramatically reduced the computational cost of 3D seismic simulations.
Spectral element methods provide high-order accuracy for wave propagation in complex geometries.
Reverse time migration (RTM) uses the full wavefield to produce high-resolution subsurface images.
Absorbing boundary conditions such as PML prevent artificial reflections from the edges of the computational domain.
Machine learning is increasingly used to automate first-break picking and velocity model building.
