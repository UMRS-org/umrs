High-performance computing enables large-scale scientific simulations that are infeasible on single machines.
MPI (Message Passing Interface) is the standard for distributed-memory parallel programming.
OpenMP provides shared-memory parallelism through compiler directives and is commonly combined with MPI.
CUDA and HIP allow researchers to harness GPU compute power for massively parallel workloads.
The Fugaku supercomputer at RIKEN uses ARM A64FX processors with high-bandwidth memory.
Job schedulers like Slurm allocate resources and manage job queues across thousands of compute nodes.
Containerization with Podman or Singularity enables reproducible software environments on HPC clusters.
I/O bottlenecks are a major challenge; parallel file systems like Lustre mitigate this.
Profiling tools such as Score-P and TAU help identify performance hotspots in parallel codes.
Energy efficiency is becoming a first-class metric alongside FLOPS for next-generation supercomputers.
