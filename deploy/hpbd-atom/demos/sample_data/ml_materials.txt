Machine Learning for Materials Science

Deep learning models are increasingly applied to materials characterization
in industrial and scientific settings. Convolutional neural networks (CNNs)
can classify defects in X-ray computed tomography (CT) scans of concrete,
metals, and composite materials with accuracy comparable to expert analysts.

Vision transformers (ViTs) and their hierarchical variants bring attention-
based architectures to materials imaging. The Hierarchical Masked
Autoencoder (HMAE) adapts the MAE pre-training paradigm to large-scale
industrial CT volumes by using quadtree-based patch decomposition. This
allows the model to allocate more patches to regions with fine structural
detail (cracks, pores, inclusions) while using coarser patches for
homogeneous regions.

Transfer learning from natural image datasets (ImageNet) to CT volumes
requires careful domain adaptation. Grayscale industrial images have
different statistical properties than RGB photographs: they have higher
dynamic range, finer texture detail at boundaries, and contain subtle
density variations that encode internal structure. Fine-tuning strategies
include:
- Progressive resizing: train at low resolution, then fine-tune at full resolution
- Masked image modeling: reconstruct randomly masked patches to learn texture
- Contrastive learning: learn embeddings that separate defective from normal regions

The Segment Anything Model (SAM) family provides zero-shot segmentation
capabilities that can be prompted with text descriptions of target features.
SAM3 extends this to 3D volumes with slice-by-slice processing and
inter-slice consistency enforcement.
