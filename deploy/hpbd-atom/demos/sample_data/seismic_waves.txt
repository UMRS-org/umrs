Seismic Wave Propagation and Numerical Methods

Seismic waves are elastic waves that propagate through the Earth following
earthquakes or controlled explosions. The two main body wave types are
P-waves (primary, compressional) and S-waves (secondary, shear). P-waves
travel faster and arrive first at seismograph stations. S-waves are slower
but carry more energy and cause greater ground motion.

Full waveform inversion (FWI) is a computational technique that iteratively
refines velocity models of the subsurface by minimizing the misfit between
observed and synthetic seismograms. The forward problem — computing synthetic
waveforms for a given velocity model — typically requires solving the elastic
wave equation using finite-difference or spectral-element methods on large 3D
grids.

Modern HPC clusters accelerate these simulations using GPU computing. A single
forward simulation on a 500x500x200 grid at 10 Hz dominant frequency can
require billions of floating-point operations. Domain decomposition with MPI
distributes the workload across thousands of GPU nodes, while halo exchange
patterns ensure correct boundary conditions between subdomains.

Key numerical methods for seismic simulation include:
- Finite-Difference Time-Domain (FDTD): simple implementation, 4th-8th order stencils
- Spectral-Element Method (SEM): hexahedral meshes, exponential convergence
- Discontinuous Galerkin (DG): handles complex geometries, local time stepping
- Boundary Element Method (BEM): efficient for homogeneous subregions
