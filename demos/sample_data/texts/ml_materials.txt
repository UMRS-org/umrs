Machine Learning for Materials Science

The intersection of machine learning and materials science is driving a
paradigm shift from trial-and-error experimentation to data-driven
discovery of advanced materials.  Neural network potentials, trained on
density-functional theory (DFT) calculations, enable molecular dynamics
simulations that are orders of magnitude faster than ab initio methods
while retaining near-DFT accuracy.

Key approaches include:
1. Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) that encode crystal structures as graphs,
   with atoms as nodes and bonds as edges.  Models like MEGNet and CGCNN
   predict formation energy, band gap, and elastic moduli directly from
   composition and structure.
2. Generative models (VAE, diffusion) for inverse materials design,
   proposing new crystal structures that satisfy target properties.
3. Transfer learning from large pre-trained models to small experimental
   datasets — critical when only tens of measurements exist.

Self-supervised methods such as Masked Auto-Encoders (MAE) learn
representations from unlabelled image data of microstructures.
Hierarchical MAE (HMAE) extends this to multi-scale microscopy:
the encoder captures local texture at the patch level while a
hierarchical decoder reconstructs global context through a
quadtree decomposition.

Segmentation models (SAM, SAM3) automate grain boundary detection,
defect identification, and phase mapping in scanning electron microscopy
(SEM) images.  When combined with embedding-based retrieval, researchers
can search large image archives by visual similarity — a capability
central to the UMRS platform's design.

Active learning further reduces annotation effort: the model queries
the oracle (human expert) only for the most informative samples,
achieving high accuracy with minimal labelling.
