Seismic Wave Propagation and Full Waveform Inversion

Seismic wave simulation is a cornerstone of computational geophysics.
The elastic wave equation governs the propagation of P-waves (compressional)
and S-waves (shear) through heterogeneous media.  Numerical discretisation
of this equation on massively parallel architectures enables full waveform
inversion (FWI), which iteratively matches observed seismograms to
synthetic data by adjusting subsurface velocity models.

Common numerical methods include:
- Finite-Difference Time-Domain (FDTD): second- or fourth-order stencils
  on regular grids; simple to implement, highly scalable on GPUs.
- Spectral Element Method (SEM): uses high-order polynomial bases on
  hexahedral meshes; naturally handles complex topography and interfaces.
- Discontinuous Galerkin (DG): combines local polynomial bases with
  numerical fluxes; excellent for adaptive meshes and heterogeneous media.

Absorbing boundary conditions (PML, C-PML) prevent artificial reflections
at the edges of the computational domain.  The adjoint-state method
computes the gradient of the misfit functional with respect to model
parameters, requiring only two wave simulations per iteration (forward
and adjoint).

Modern FWI workflows on HPC clusters exploit domain decomposition with
MPI and GPU acceleration via CUDA or HIP.  A single 3-D inversion at
exploration scale may require O(10^4) CPU-hours.  Reproducibility of
such runs is ensured through containerised environments (Apptainer)
and deterministic random-number seeding.

Key challenges remain:
1. Cycle-skipping: initial models far from the global minimum trap the
   optimiser in local minima.
2. Cross-talk between P- and S-wave velocities in elastic FWI.
3. Scaling 3-D elastic FWI to the crustal or global scale.
4. Incorporating anisotropy and attenuation consistently.
