Machine Learning Applications in Geophysics

Deep learning has transformed seismic data interpretation and geophysical modeling over the past decade. Traditional methods relied heavily on manual picking and rule-based algorithms, but modern neural network architectures can automatically extract features from raw seismic data with unprecedented accuracy.

Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) are widely used for seismic facies classification and fault detection. By treating seismic sections as images, 2D and 3D CNNs can identify geological structures including faults, salt bodies, and stratigraphic horizons. U-Net architectures, originally developed for biomedical image segmentation, have been successfully adapted for seismic interpretation tasks.

Recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and their variants, particularly Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, are applied to sequential seismic data analysis. They excel at tasks involving temporal patterns, such as seismic event detection, phase picking, and earthquake early warning systems.

Physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) represent a paradigm shift by embedding physical constraints directly into the learning process. By incorporating the wave equation as a loss function penalty, PINNs can solve forward and inverse problems while respecting fundamental physics. This approach reduces the need for large labeled datasets and produces physically consistent results.

Generative adversarial networks (GANs) are used for seismic data augmentation and super-resolution. They can generate realistic synthetic seismograms for training when real data is scarce, and enhance the resolution of legacy surveys without reacquisition.

Transfer learning enables models trained on one geological setting to be adapted for another with limited local data. This is particularly valuable in exploration geophysics, where labeled data from new prospect areas is typically sparse. Pre-trained models from well-studied basins can be fine-tuned with small amounts of target domain data.

Graph neural networks (GNNs) are emerging as powerful tools for modeling subsurface flow in porous media and analyzing seismic network data. They naturally handle irregular spatial structures and can capture complex spatial dependencies that regular grids cannot represent efficiently.
